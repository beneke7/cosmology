# Primordial deuterium and an independent BBN baryon prior

**Cutoff:** 2026-09-25. **Status:** literature audit; no BBN likelihood was run locally. The listed PDFs were downloaded and hashed locally; the cited primary papers and relevant tables/equations were checked in full text, and the Launders Table I entry was visually inspected in the PDF.

## Recommendation

For a late-time-only model whose early thermal history is standard, use the official PDG 2025 standard-BBN result as a broad independent cross-check/prior:

\[
\omega_b \equiv \Omega_bh^2 = 0.02205\pm0.00043\quad(1\sigma).
\]

This is PDG Eq. (24.6), a D+\(^4\)He concordance inference in SBBN, dominated by D/H. It uses light-element absorption/emission observations and nuclear/weak-interaction physics, not DESI BAO. It can therefore be included alongside DESI DR2 BAO without BAO double counting, provided the model preserves the standard BBN history. Do not substitute a CMB+BAO-derived \(\omega_b\) for this prior, and do not combine this prior with the D/H and helium likelihoods from which it was derived.

Treat tighter PRIMAT results as a **model/rate-specific sensitivity case**, not the default. Poulin et al. (arXiv:2607.20635v1) report D/H-only PRIMAT \(\omega_b\simeq0.0219\pm0.00022\) for Cooke et al.'s \(10^5\mathrm{D/H}=2.527\pm0.030\) (main-text Eqs. 1–2); when replacing that abundance with the PDG value 2.508±0.030, their Appendix A/Table II gives a *joint D+LBT-helium* BBN posterior \(100\omega_b=2.2017^{+0.0221}_{-0.0217}\), or \(\omega_b=0.022017^{+0.000221}_{-0.000217}\). The posterior is not D/H-only. The PRIMAT-specific narrow result is useful as a sensitivity bracket, but it depends on the adopted nuclear rates and helium likelihood; the preprint remains v1 and its reproduction files are restricted (details below).

For an explicit wider nuclear-rate sensitivity, Schöneberg's 2024 update (arXiv:2401.15054v3, §5 Eqs. 5.1–5.2) recommends \(0.02218\pm0.00055\) with the PDG light-element inputs and \(0.02231\pm0.00055\) after substituting the then-latest Kislitsyn D/H result, using PRyMordial with NACRE-II experimental rates and conservative rate marginalization. The latter abundance compilation predates the PDG 2025 update. In the same paper, alternative PRIMAT-rate calculations yield substantially lower centers (e.g. PDG-input row in Table 2: \(0.02166\pm0.00039\)). These alternatives share abundance data and differ in rate assumptions: **do not average or multiply them as independent priors**. Use them to expose theory dependence.

**Do not use any SBBN \(\omega_b\) Gaussian as a plug-in prior for an IVS model if IVS changes the expansion or thermal history during BBN.** The mapping from D/H to \(\omega_b\) is conditional on \(H(T)\), weak freeze-out, nuclear rates, and neutron lifetime. A modified expansion changes neutron/proton freeze-out and/or the time available for deuterium burning, shifting the D/H–\(\omega_b\) relation. For an IVS model active around BBN (including the late D-burning window around \(T\sim0.03\) MeV), fit the measured abundance likelihood and recompute BBN with that model's expansion history. A model confined to late times, leaving BBN unchanged, may use the SBBN prior.

## Observational record and consistency

PDG 2025 §24.3/Table 24.1 lists 12 high-precision quasar-absorber measurements. Its recommended \(10^6\mathrm{D/H}=25.08\pm0.29\) follows a weighted mean 25.08±0.27 with \(\chi^2/(N-1)=1.160\) and uncertainty inflation \(S=1.08\) (Eq. 24.2). The table shows the twelve values spanning roughly 19.68–26.18 in the same units; the low J1444+2919 value has a large asymmetric error. PDG finds no correlation with metallicity, redshift, or H I column, and estimates only 0.1–1% astration in these low-metallicity systems. The extra-dispersion correction is modest, but the inflated \(\chi^2\) is a reminder that quoted per-absorber errors do not capture every systematic. PDG publishes no cross-absorber covariance matrix.

A targeted search through the cutoff found no later individual high-redshift absorber determination than the 2024 studies below; the PDG 2025 twelve-system compilation is the latest broad official observational summary identified.

The two latest precision absorber papers identified in this audit are consistent with that compilation:

Cooke, Pettini & Steidel (2018) report a homogeneous seven-system result \(10^5\mathrm{D/H}=2.527\pm0.030\) (Eqs. 9–10; their new Q1243+307 sightline measurement is log(D/H)=−4.622±0.015). Their selected sample and fitting methodology are deliberately homogeneous; their §4.3 tests intrinsic scatter and finds zero scatter preferred. The individual sightline analysis models continuum, blends, velocity structure, and zero level. They note neighboring pixel correlations are not included in their spectral \(\chi^2\); this is not a published cross-absorber covariance. Their historical value differs from the PDG 2025 mean by only about 0.46σ. Kislitsyn's different 17-object compilation is also close to PDG and its newest individual point is only about 1.4σ low. Thus no compelling absorber-to-absorber tension or trend is established; the larger practical issue is analysis/rate dependence, not a statistically significant outlier.

The SBBN–CMB comparisons are likewise not a decisive contradiction at the recommended broad-prior level. PDG Eq. (24.7) gives Planck TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing \(\omega_b=0.02237\pm0.00015\); the difference from its Eq. (24.6) BBN value is about 0.7σ if errors are treated as independent. Poulin et al. quote SPA CMB \(0.022398\pm0.000095\) and SPA+DESI \(0.022478\pm0.000091\); their updated PRIMAT D+He posterior near 0.02202 is about 1.6σ and 2.0σ low, respectively. The BBN data are independent of DESI BAO, but a quoted SPA+DESI posterior is not an independent BAO-free baryon prior.

## Theory uncertainty and code access

Near the standard solution Poulin et al. state \(\mathrm{D/H}\propto\omega_b^{-1.65}\) (Introduction). Their PRIMAT v0.3.2 likelihood adds tabulated nuclear-rate and neutron-lifetime abundance uncertainty in quadrature with observation error (Analysis §III.1, likelihood Eq. 14); the total D-only density uncertainty in Eq. 2 is about 0.00022. As a transparent, **reconstructed approximation** rather than a published component table, linearizing this power law around \(\mathrm{D/H}=2.508\) gives an observational-only \(\sigma_{\omega,obs}\simeq(0.0220/1.65)(0.030/2.508)=0.00016\); quadrature subtraction then leaves roughly 0.00015 for the combined PRIMAT nuclear-rate/neutron-lifetime term. The exact split is not tabulated there. PDG Eq. 24.6 also supplies only a combined error, not a nuclear-versus-observational decomposition.

There is independent evidence that the network/rate choice matters. Launders, Giovanetti & Liu (arXiv:2604.16600v1) use LINX and Gaussian-process fits to the measured \(d(d,n)^3\mathrm{He}\), \(d(d,p)t\), and \(d(p,\gamma)^3\mathrm{He}\) S-factors. At Planck \(\omega_b\), their Table 2 finds \(10^5\mathrm{D/H}=2.444\pm0.037\) for PRIMAT, 2.512±0.042 for PArthENoPE, and 2.520±0.104 for PRyMordial/YOF; their GP prediction is 2.442±0.040. The authors identify the two D+D rates as less certain than D(p,γ). Their rate-data covariance prescription is explicit in Methods §III.4, Eqs. 16–17: off-diagonal covariance represents common experimental normalization uncertainty within each experiment; a full covariance is used where supplied. These are forward D/H predictions, not baryon priors, and they show why a narrow mapping cannot hide nuclear-rate choices.

The reference conventions are not perfectly uniform: PDG's SBBN section states \(N_\nu=3\) and one free parameter \(\eta\) (§24.4), while Poulin et al.'s baseline uses \(N_{\rm eff}^{\rm SM}=3.044\) (§II, Eq. 5). Their results are both standard-physics baselines in their respective implementations, but should not be treated as identical calculations.

**Erratum-like internal inconsistency to preserve:** Launders et al. Table 1 (PDF p. 6) prints the Planck row as \(100\omega_b=2.337\pm0.015\). The same paper's Eq. (3), PDF p. 1, says \(\omega_b=0.02237\pm0.00015\), which converts to \(100\omega_b=2.237\pm0.015\). The PDF table itself was visually inspected and really does say 2.337; this audit treats it as a likely typo and does not use it as an input. Their stated D/H predictions and Table 2 remain the relevant cross-network comparison.

The full arXiv PDFs cited below are openly accessible. Generic [PRIMAT](https://github.com/CyrilPitrou/primat) and [primat_tools](https://github.com/CyrilPitrou/primat_tools) GitHub repositories are public. Poulin et al. describe PRIMAT as Python v0.3.2 and `primat_tools` as the Cobaya likelihood/profile wrapper (Analysis §III.1). However, the article's arXiv v1 says the exact code/data/notebooks for reproducing its figures will be released upon acceptance (Conclusion). A Zenodo record, [10.5281/zenodo.21494819](https://zenodo.org/records/21494819), now describes chains, profiles, YAMLs, scripts, and the manuscript snapshot, but at the audit cutoff its record states the files are **restricted** and shows 0-byte data. Hence the exact numerical reproduction bundle is not publicly downloadable, even though the general solver and wrapper are. This audit did not attempt to access restricted files.

## Checks and limits

- **Reproduced arithmetic:** converting PDG Eq. (24.5) with \(\omega_b\simeq\eta_{10}/274\) gives 0.02204±0.00043, consistent with the printed 0.02205±0.00043 after rounding/conversion precision. The direct PDG sample's printed mean and scale factor were checked against Table 24.1; the table's rounded asymmetric entries do not exactly reproduce its unrounded weighted fit.
- **Independence check:** the PDG SBBN abundance inference is not built from BAO or CMB+BAO; it is suitable as a separate likelihood factor beside DESI DR2 only if early BBN physics is standard. Correlation with unrelated CMB-derived constraints is a different matter and must be assessed from what those constraints include.
- **No local inference claimed:** there was no numerical PRIMAT run, posterior reconstruction, or direct fit of a D/H likelihood in this audit. The uncertainty split above is an explicitly labeled linearized estimate, not reproduced code output.
- **No absorber covariance claimed:** the reviewed compilations provide per-system errors and weighted sample statistics, but no cross-absorber systematic covariance matrix. PDG's scale-factor inflation handles observed scatter empirically; it is not a full shared-systematic covariance model.
- **Stop/reopen rule:** stop this audit at the 2026-09-25 cutoff. Reopen if a new high-quality absorber measurement/compilation or a material new low-energy D+D rate determination appears, if a journal version or downloadable/unrestricted reproduction bundle changes the status of arXiv:2607.20635, or before applying a prior to any model that changes BBN-era expansion/thermodynamics.
