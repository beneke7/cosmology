# NGC 3198 resolved baryon-source audit

**Finding — feasible for a resolved H I surface-density input; conditionally feasible for a 3D baryon source.** Public, target-specific moment-0/column-density maps exist in both THINGS and HALOGAS. They measure the gas distribution directly from 21-cm emission, independently of spherical inversion of SPARC rotation components. The maps constrain projected H I column density, not a unique three-dimensional density; vertical profiles, stellar mass-to-light ratio, and non-H I baryons still require explicit assumptions.

## Geometry and conversion assumptions

THINGS adopts D=13.8 Mpc, i=72°, and disk PA=215° for NGC 3198. HALOGAS uses D=13.8±1.5 Mpc, describes the inclination as about 70°, and uses PA=213°; its modeled H I maps become marginally inconsistent for inclination shifts of about 2°. A reasonable nominal geometry is therefore i≈71°, PA≈214°, with at least a ±2° geometry sensitivity check and distance uncertainty propagated (mass scales as D²). At this distance 1″ is about 67 pc.

For the optically thin 21-cm conversion, THINGS gives N_HI = 1.823e18 × sum(T_B × Δv), with T_B = 6.07e5 × S/(beam_major × beam_minor). Its map is in Jy beam⁻¹ m s⁻¹, so the integrated intensity must be converted from m s⁻¹ to km s⁻¹. Convert line-of-sight column to a thin-disk face-on surface density with the adopted inclination, then multiply by 1.36 for helium and metals. This does not correct H I self-absorption. THINGS reports about 5% absolute flux-calibration uncertainty and does not apply an optical-depth correction; the paper notes that self-absorption can lower inferred H I masses. That caveat is especially relevant at NGC 3198’s relatively high inclination (~70°).

The HALOGAS NGC 3198 paper measures total H I flux 239.9 Jy km s⁻¹ and M_HI=1.08e10 M⊙ at 13.8 Mpc, about 6% above the THINGS value; it attributes the difference partly to calibration and partly to extraplanar gas. The higher-resolution cube beam is 18.9″×13.6″; the more diffuse-gas model used a 35.2″×33.5″ beam. At 30″ the paper reaches roughly 1e19 atoms cm⁻². It finds a preferred thick component with scale height about 3 kpc and about 15% of H I mass, while a 0.2 kpc sech² layer was only the initial thin-disk modeling assumption. The thick fraction and scale height are uncertain, and a 2D map alone cannot separate the two components.

## Minimal physical source recommendation

Use the HALOGAS HR column-density map as the primary resolved gas measurement, with THINGS natural moment 0 as an independent cross-check. Deproject into disk-plane annuli using the above geometry, azimuthally average to define Σ_HI(R), mask the companion VV 834 and the paper’s separate faint off-disk feature, and use the same radial H I profile for a thin component and (as a clearly labeled alternative) a HALOGAS-motivated thick component. The direct axisymmetric source can be written

`rho_HI+He(R,z) = 1.36 × Sigma_HI(R) × [0.85 p_thin(z; h_thin) + 0.15 p_thick(z; h_thick)]`

where each vertical profile is normalized to unit integral; bracket thin-only against the uncertain thick-layer alternative. Pair this with the S4G P5 old-stellar map converted to stellar surface density using a stated M/L (the S4G color map supports a color-dependent alternative), then assign and vary an explicit stellar vertical profile. Convolve/rebin the stellar map to the gas-map resolution before combining. H₂, ionized gas, and any remaining stellar M/L or thickness uncertainty must be added or explicitly bounded; neither H I product is a complete baryon census.

This is a defensible parametric, axisymmetric 3D baryon source independent of spherical inversion of SPARC velocities. It is not a direct measurement of the full 3D source: the vertical profiles and omitted baryon components remain nuisance assumptions, and azimuthal averaging removes spiral-arm asymmetry. A later fit should test thin-only versus thin+thick gas, geometry, distance, opacity, and stellar M/L sensitivities before interpreting any TG result.

## Access, licensing, and cache

Both H I FITS files were retrieved from their official, target-specific public links on 2026-09-25; no cubes or survey-wide archives were fetched. The THINGS page/paper provides public access but no explicit separate data license. The HALOGAS Zenodo record’s license field is blank; it explicitly requests citation of Heald et al. (2011) and the supplied HALOGAS acknowledgment, and the NGC 3198 analysis paper should also be cited. IRSA states that most public-hosted data have no usage restrictions; for S4G it requests citation of Sheth et al. (2010), Muñoz-Mateos et al. (2013), Querejeta et al. (2015), Watkins et al. (2022), and the dataset DOI.

The two acquired products total 8,519,040 bytes. The project’s documented 20 GiB selected-cache cap had 21,305,342,268 bytes remaining before this task; after counting both maps it has 21,296,823,228 bytes remaining. No shared source catalog or campaign state was edited. No modeling or TG fit was performed.
