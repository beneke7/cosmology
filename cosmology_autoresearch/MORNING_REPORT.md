## What looks promising, and what is not

The genuinely promising part is the cross-checking strategy: matched nulls show how often the search alone imitates a fit gain; redshift holdouts show where predictions succeed or fail; and frozen shapes let us compare probes without multiplying their likelihoods. Here, the same Dovekie Δχ² gain appears in 25% of matched ΛCDM mocks, while the wider domain leaves the optimum unchanged. The completed local response audit finds the interaction response is strongly degenerate with Ωm after profiling the intercept: only about 6% of the post-intercept local g information remains after projecting Ωm. Its near-zero residual projection onto that conditional direction is expected at a joint fitted minimum and is an optimizer/GLS consistency check, not independent evidence against the model. The matched-null result—not that residual alignment—is the direct reason not to interpret this restricted SN-only gain as a signal. The next meaningful step is a source-faithful perturbation/CMB likelihood or a clearly bounded feasibility gate for an independent implementation—not more flexible distance curves. Recent primary literature likewise warns that interaction preference depends on DE trajectory and Bayesian evidence can favor ΛCDM. [Yang et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05660), [Wang, Yu & Wu (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03062).

The low-effort parameter tweaks are not promising as standalone claims. Curvature's gain is routinely reproduced by the flat null. IVS's BAO gain is common in its own matched null and it fails the high-z predictive stress. CPL has the largest exploratory profile gain among these screens, but its tail and domain sensitivity are insufficient to call it a result. Further arbitrary BAO-only curves, decimal polishing, or GPU acceleration for a three-second profile would be stupid-simple in the unhelpful sense: they add little information. The profile now did use the workstation where it mattered (20-core grid and 3.11× measured parallel speedup), and completed quickly; the GPU remained idle for a good reason, not because the benchmark was skipped. The independent large-surface benchmark already validates that the 5090 is useful when there is a sufficiently large batched task.
